## 1. The question

From the P9 spec, §8. Can a clean-room reimplementation of OpenIrisDPI run as a headless
expcontroller service, and in which language for the core? It must find P1 (the corneal
reflection) and P4 (the reflection off the back of the lens):

- in two BFS-U3-16S2M-CS cameras at 500 Hz;
- at about 1 ms per frame (the paper reports 1.1 ± 0.1 ms, median ± IQR);
- with precision comparable to the paper's in-vivo white-noise RMS of 0.39′ (azimuth)
  and 0.44′ (elevation).

S5 consumes the pupil center, P1 and P4 per eye. Its gaze signal is P1 − P4 (`CR1 − CR4`),
and expcontroller calibrates it itself.

## 2. Answer

**Recommendation: build it, on conditions (§10). Use a C++ core.**

*Updated with §7b (the tutorial recording). The recommendation stands. What changed: the
real-data bullet below, and §10's conditions 1 and 2.*

- **Speed is not the obstacle (dev machine).** The published algorithm, implemented from
  the paper, takes **0.24 ms per 720 × 450 frame** (C++, median, one or two cameras in
  parallel). The 99.9th percentile is ≤ 0.32 ms when the loop runs back to back. The same
  code in numba takes 0.34 ms. At full frame (1440 × 1080) it is 0.63 ms (C++) and 1.07 ms
  (numba). In the quieter first run two cameras did not slow each other; under the second
  run's heavier load they did (C++ 0.23 → 0.28 ms). Tails under the machine's background load
  reach tens of ms in some paced runs, in both cores and often in both camera threads of
  the same run. That is this laptop, and it says nothing about a tuned Linux rig.
- **The algorithm is not the precision bottleneck. P4 brightness is.** On synthetic frames,
  the P1 − P4 noise is **0.018–0.019 px per axis** at the P4 brightness that the paper's
  own figures suggest (P4 SNR ≈ 76). Under a stated optical model (§5.2) that is about
  **0.12′ azimuth and 0.16′ elevation**, roughly a third of the paper's in-vivo 0.39′/0.44′.
  The paper's number is reached when P4's peak SNR falls to about 25. Real P4 SNR at
  940 nm, with the camera's QE below 10 %, is the unknown that decides precision, and only
  bring-up can measure it.
- **Real data (§7b) confirms that P4 carries the noise, and finds a second limit.** These
  numbers are from OpenIrisDPI's own output on its tutorial recording:
  - P1 − P4's white-noise floor in fixation is **0.027–0.034 px per axis**.
  - The spike's code shows that noise at a **P4 SNR of about 40–50**. That is an
    inference, not a brightness measurement.
  - At the recording's own gain it is 0.27–0.32′, if its targets are in degrees; their unit
    is not stated.
  - But most of the real fixation jitter is not white. A P4-only component at about
    40–160 Hz makes the sample-to-sample RMS (0.089–0.119 px) **2.1–3.2 × what white
    noise at the floor would give**.
  - The synthetic model has no such term, and positions alone cannot say whether it is the
    eye or an artifact.
- **The paper is enough to build from.** Algorithm 1 plus Fig. 2's settings specify the
  method. Our thresholded centroid reproduces the paper's own Fig. B.1 simulation to within
  6 % (§6.3). What the paper leaves open (§8) is decidable. Two of its gaps matter for
  precision; neither is a speed problem:
  1. The published algorithm has no validity or blink logic. On partial blinks it returns
     a confident, wrong P4 (§7.5).
  2. P4 within about 20 px of the pupil edge is pulled 7–9 px toward the iris at the
     published R_P4 = 15 (§7.4).
- **A clean-room build is feasible, so the license question has an answer.** Everything
  here was written from the paper and general literature (§3), so the code is free of
  GPL/AGPL derivation and compatible with this repository's Apache-2.0.
- **Nothing here compares against OpenIrisDPI on the same frames.** §7b measures
  OpenIrisDPI's output alone, because the bundle has no video. The same-frames comparison
  is still the first condition.

## 4. What the paper specifies

Algorithm 1, bioRxiv v2, Appendix B (the paper's line numbers):

| Stage | Lines | Operation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6–7 | Crop to D; Gaussian blur, radius R_b |
| 2 | 8–13 | Threshold the blurred image below T_pup; take the centroid of all such pixels; circular mask of radius R_pup around it; Laplacian of (threshold ∧ mask); edge = Laplacian > 0; convex hull of the edge pixels |
| 3 | 14 | Fit an ellipse to the hull (pupil) |
| 4 | 15–18 | Pixels > T_cr inside the mask; their centroid; ROI of radius R_cr; CR = thresholded centroid (subtract T_cr, clip at 0) |
| 5 | 19–25 | Mask out R_cr around the CR; fill the hull and erode by R_erode; argmax inside; ROI of radius R_P4; P4 = thresholded centroid **with threshold T_pup** |

## 5. The synthetic model (`synth.py`)

Frames have a dark elliptical pupil with an anti-aliased edge, a textured iris, skin, and a
saturated P1. P4 is dimmer and small. There is optional P2, sensor noise (read noise plus
shot noise), 8-bit quantization and clipping. Reflections are pixel-integrated Gaussians,
so ground truth is exact to sub-pixel. Pixel (r, c) covers [c ± 0.5, r ± 0.5].

**A correction to the brief.** The brief described P4 as "larger, blurrier" than P1. Wu et
al. 2023 describe P4 as "smaller and weaker than P1", and the paper's own figures show it
smaller than the saturated P1. The model follows the sources and sweeps P4's σ from 1.5 to
3.5 px. Precision turned out insensitive across that range (§7.1).

### 5.2 Optical model for arcmin (stated and ASSUMED, arithmetic shown)

**Magnification** (thin lens, f = 100 mm, 570 mm taken as the object distance):

- m = 100 / (570 − 100) = 0.2128;
- one pixel covers 3.45 / 0.2128 = **16.2 µm** of eye;
- if 570 mm were the whole object-to-sensor track instead, m = 0.294, one pixel covers
  11.7 µm, and every arcmin figure below shrinks by 0.72.

**DPI gain:** 2.4 µm of P1–P4 motion per arcmin (Wu 2023, human model eye). The paraxial
check d sin Δ, with d = 7.7 mm, gives 2.24.

**Conversion:**

- azimuth: 16.2 / 2.4 = **6.76′ per px**;
- elevation: 16.2 / (2.4 × cos 35°) = **8.25′ per px**;
- for a macaque eye, ASSUMED 0.85 × human d, **7.95′ and 9.70′ per px** (a sensitivity row,
  UNVERIFIED).

**The paper's in-vivo 0.39′ / 0.44′** therefore corresponds to:

- **0.058 / 0.053 px** at human gain;
- 0.049 / 0.045 px at the ASSUMED macaque gain.

The paper's model-eye figure (0.27′ RMS, static for 10 min) includes low-frequency
vibration and is not a white-noise figure.

## 6. The method as implemented

### 6.1 Two cores, one algorithm

- **Core (a):** `dpi_numba.py`, Python + numba.
- **Core (b):** `dpi_core.cpp`, C++17 with a C ABI, called through `dpi_cpp.py` (ctypes)
  or from `bench_cpp.cpp` (no Python).

Both follow Algorithm 1 statement for statement, allocate nothing per frame, and share
integer arithmetic. The blur uses integer weights 18/63/94/63/18. Two accelerations are
exact rather than approximate:

- **Hull.** The convex hull of all Laplacian edge pixels equals the hull of each row's
  leftmost and rightmost edge pixel, and those follow from each row's extreme thresholded
  pixels. Stage 2 is then one pass over the search circle (0.08 ms in C++, §7.2).
- **Erosion.** Filling the convex hull and eroding it by R_erode is the set of points at
  least R_erode inside every hull edge, which is one x-interval per row. This is exact
  geometrically. A discrete-kernel erosion would differ by up to a pixel at the boundary,
  a choice the paper leaves open (§8).

### 6.2 Agreement (`check_agreement.py`, `agreement.json`)

The check covers 27 frames: rotations, blinks, half-blinks, a pupil against the image
border, and four noise levels.

- **numba against C++:** the largest difference in any output is **0.0**; they are bit
  identical.
- **Against a literal numpy/scipy reference** (a full 4-neighbor Laplacian of the masked
  binary image, qhull over every edge pixel, numpy's `eig`):
  - identical hulls on every frame;
  - identical CR and P4;
  - pupil center within 4 × 10⁻¹³ px.
- **Under Linux** (Debian aarch64 container, GCC 14.2) the same check agrees
  (`linux_container_agreement.txt`). That checks correctness only, not timing.
- **The check can fail** (`check_agreement.py --controls`, `agreement_controls.json`):
  - C++ given T_pup 31 instead of 30: caught (a detection differs);
  - an 8-neighbor Laplacian in the reference: the hull differs on 24 of 27 frames. On the
    19 non-blink frames the pupil center moves ≤ 0.05 px, and P1 and P4 are unaffected.
  - C++ given R_erode 7 or R_P4 14: caught, but only through the blink frames (up to
    2.0 px and 0.59 px). On the 19 non-blink frames they change **nothing**. Those settings
    matter only where P4 is near the hull boundary (§7.4) or is not P4 at all (§7.5).

### 6.3 The thresholded centroid reproduces the paper's Fig. B.1 (`figb1_check.py`)

The paper's minima were read by eye from its log-scale plot, so they carry about ±15 %.

|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | ~0.27 px @ ~+2 SD | 0.331 @ +3.0 | 0.287 @ +2.5 |
| 10 | ~0.115 @ ~+2.5 | 0.119 @ +3.0 | 0.118 @ +2.5 |

## 7. Results

### 7.1 Precision (`bench_precision.py`, `precision.json`)

Numbers are P1 − P4 errors, estimate minus truth, per axis (x = azimuth, y = elevation).

- **"Noise SD"** is the pooled within-position SD over noise realizations. It is the
  analogue of the paper's white-noise RMS.
- **"Bias spread"** is the SD, across 20 positions with independent sub-pixel phases, of
  each position's mean error. It captures pixel locking.
- **P4 SNR** is P4's peak above the pupil background, divided by the noise SD at the pupil
  level, before blurring. That is Fig. B.1's definition.
- Arcmin columns use §5.2's human-eye gain.

| P4 SNR (noise sweep) | P1 noise SD px | P4 noise SD px | P1−P4 noise SD px | P1−P4 bias spread px | P1−P4 arcmin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 152 | 0.005 / 0.005 | 0.010 / 0.010 | **0.011 / 0.011** | 0.016 / 0.015 | 0.07′ / 0.09′ |
| 76 (base) | 0.007 / 0.007 | 0.017 / 0.018 | **0.018 / 0.019** | 0.019 / 0.015 | 0.12′ / 0.16′ |
| 38 | 0.011 / 0.011 | 0.034 / 0.033 | **0.036 / 0.035** | 0.014 / 0.018 | 0.24′ / 0.29′ |
| 19 | 0.018 / 0.018 | 0.073 / 0.068 | **0.075 / 0.071** | 0.019 / 0.014 | 0.51′ / 0.58′ |
| 10 | 0.032 / 0.034 | 0.134 / 0.132 | **0.137 / 0.135** | 0.019 / 0.028 | 0.93′ / 1.11′ |

- **P4 dominates.** Its error is 2.5–4 × P1's at every SNR, so the P1 − P4 noise is P4's
  noise.
- **The P4 amplitude sweep agrees** (P1 kept at 80 × P4): 0.064 / 0.060 px at SNR 30 and
  0.014 / 0.014 px at SNR 110. Lowering P4's brightness and raising the noise are
  interchangeable.
- **P4 size, 1.5–3.5 px, does not matter:** 0.019 ± 0.001 px throughout.
- **Pixel locking sits at 0.01–0.04 px in every sweep.** It is comparable to the noise at high
  SNR. It is a smooth trueness error, which per-session calibration will partly absorb, not
  white noise.
- **Eye rotation over a 5 × 5 grid, ±8°:** noise SD 0.017–0.020 px wherever P4 is found.
- **Head translation of ±30 px (±0.49 mm):** noise SD 0.016–0.021 px and mean bias
  ≤ 0.02 px. P1 − P4 is translation-invariant, as it should be. Only lateral translation
  was modeled; §9 covers depth.
- **Where precision reaches the paper's figure.** Between SNR 38 and 19 this core passes
  through 0.058 / 0.053 px, the paper's in-vivo figure at §5.2's human gain. Scaling as
  1/SNR puts that at a P4 SNR of about 24–27.

**This is not like for like with the paper.** Synthetic frames have Gaussian reflections,
perfect focus, no tear film, no tremor, no vibration and a known noise model. The paper's
WN-RMS is estimated from the spectral power above 200 Hz of real signals from macaques.

So the synthetic result is a **lower bound on noise at a given P4 SNR**, not a prediction
of in-vivo precision.

**Reading the speed results:**

- **The CPU cost fits the budget with a margin of about 8.** At 500 Hz a camera allows
  2 ms per frame, and C++ uses about 0.24 ms of it (12 % of one core). In run 1 two cameras
  ran in parallel at unchanged medians.
- **C++ is 1.4 × faster than numba at 720 × 450, and 1.7 × at full frame**, on identical
  outputs. In the busier second run, numba's median rose to 0.43–0.65 ms. C++ called from
  C++ held 0.23–0.28 ms unpaced (0.38 ms paced, one camera).
- **The tails belong to this laptop.** Frames over 10 ms were 0.001–0.004 % at 720 × 450
  and 0.01–0.04 % at full frame. The worst stall was 61 ms, and both camera threads of that
  run recorded one (60.99 and 60.81 ms), which points at the machine rather than the code.
  Tails were worse when paced, where the core idles between frames. They say nothing about
  a Linux rig with isolated cores and real-time priority. The paper's "2 % ≥ 10 ms, max 50 ms" is likewise a
  statement about its Windows PC.
- **The comparison with the paper's 1.1 ms is not like for like** (different CPU, OS,
  library and timing boundary). What does carry over is that the algorithm needs well
  under a millisecond of CPU per frame.

### 7.3 Detection across the working range

- **P4 is found everywhere on the ±8° grid except az −4°, el −8°.** There P4 comes within
  R_cr = 29 px of P1 and line 19's mask removes it.
- **The minimum P1–P4 separation lies between 20 and 28 px.** P4 is lost at 20 px and found
  at 28, via its tail outside the mask, with an unbiased centroid. R_cr sets the minimum.

### 7.4 Hard cases

**P4 near the pupil edge.** The published line 25 centers P4 with threshold T_pup over a
square ROI. Iris pixels (75 DN) are above T_pup (30), so an ROI that reaches the iris pulls
P4 toward it. Mean P1 − P4 error, x / y px, P4 d px inside the edge:

| d | R_P4 = 15 (Fig. 2) | R_P4 = 6 | ROI masked to the hull (not published) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | −6.9 / +6.6 (83 % found) | −0.12 / +0.11 (90 %) | −0.45 / +0.51 (93 %) |
| 9 | −9.1 / +8.1 | −0.04 / +0.05 | −0.64 / +0.61 |
| 12 | −9.4 / +9.7 | −0.00 / −0.01 | −0.65 / +0.70 |
| 15 | −8.2 / +8.6 | +0.00 / −0.01 | −0.51 / +0.53 |
| 20 | −0.8 / +0.9 | +0.01 / +0.00 | −0.21 / +0.23 |
| 30 | +0.01 / +0.00 | +0.01 / +0.00 | +0.01 / +0.00 |

P4 at d = 3 px is never found, because R_erode = 8 excludes it.

- **R_P4 = 6 removes the bias down to about 9 px from the edge.** It is an operator
  setting and changes nothing in the algorithm. Precision elsewhere is unchanged.
- **Masking the ROI to the hull helps less**, because the blurred edge ramp lies inside the
  hull.
- **Whether OpenIrisDPI shows the same bias on real eyes is unknown.** The literal
  pseudocode does. The paper says P4 is visible to about 10° before the iris obscures it,
  but not how precise it is near that limit. This is a same-frames question.

**P1 near the pupil edge.** At +8° elevation with ±4–8° azimuth, P1 in this geometry sits
on the lower pupil boundary. The near-saturation plateau is then asymmetric (iris on one
side), which biases the CR up to 0.19 px (1.6′ in elevation). P-CR trackers face the same
effect.

**P2 beside P1** (1 % of P1, 6 px away). The CR shifts about 0.11 px toward P2, nearly
constant across gaze in this model, where P2's offset is fixed. The paper says this bias
is "a deterministic function of viewing angle" that calibration absorbs, and this is
consistent with that.

### 7.5 Blinks: the published algorithm has no failure logic

300 frames per closure; "closure" is the fraction of the pupil's height the lid covers.

| Lid closure | Pupil reported | CR reported | P4 reported | Reported P4 > 2 px from true P4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 (closed) | 25 % | 9 % | 25 % | 100 % |
| 0.5 (P4 covered) | 100 % | 82 % | 100 % | 100 % (median 70 px) |
| 0.3 (P4 still visible) | 100 % | 39 % | 100 % | 100 % (median 71 px) |

**The mechanism:**

1. Dark lashes fall below T_pup and inflate the convex hull.
2. The hull then includes saturated glints on the lid margin.
3. Line 23's argmax picks a glint. Sometimes the CR is a glint as well, and the real P1
   becomes "P4".

Every one of these is a confident, wrong sample. Our minimum-pupil-pixel gate catches three
quarters of full closures and no partial ones. In S5's design these frames must reach
`taskd` as **invalid**, feeding the staleness path, never as a gaze sample.

How OpenIrisDPI flags them is not in the paper. On its tutorial recording it does not flag
them (§7b.2–§7b.3):

- `DataQuality` is 100 on every frame;
- a P4 is reported on every frame where the pupil is lost, a median 321–337 px from the
  last usable P4;
- the 50 ms either side of each pupil loss carry unflagged P4 jumps.

### 7.6 Choices the paper leaves open, tested

| Variant (P4 SNR 76 / 19) | P1 − P4 noise SD x / y px |
|---|---|
| Published (P4 threshold = T_pup) | 0.019 / 0.019 — 0.071 / 0.070 |
| No blur (R_b = 0) | 0.023 / 0.026 — **38 / 63 (gross failures)** |
| P4 threshold 20 | 0.019 / 0.018 — 0.066 / 0.066 |

The blur is essential: without it, at SNR 19, argmax lands on noise. The choice of P4

### 7b.5 Jitter in fixation

**Method (`measure_recording.py`):**

1. Only usable frames (§7b.2).
2. Velocity is the 11-frame (22 ms) least-squares slope of P1 − P4 on each axis.
3. A frame is in fixation when:
   - its speed is under **30 px/s** (about 5 target units/s at §7b.6's gain);
   - all 11 frames are usable;
   - no pupil loss lies within 100 ms.
4. Each fixation run is cut into **non-overlapping 100-frame (200 ms) windows**: 6,942 left
   and 6,995 right, with 82–83 % of frames in fixation.
5. Per window and axis:
   - the SD;
   - the RMS of successive differences ("step RMS");
   - a **white-noise floor**, √(mean PSD above 200 Hz × fs/2). This is the analogue of the
     paper's WN-RMS as §7.1 describes it (spectral power above 200 Hz);
   - the lag-1 autocorrelation of the steps, which is −0.5 for white noise.
6. A self-test on signals of known noise must pass first (the scripts' README).

**Medians across windows, px:**

| Median (IQR for P1 − P4) | Left SD | Left step RMS | Left floor | Right SD | Right step RMS | Right floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 x | 0.267 | 0.046 | 0.011 | 0.297 | 0.046 | 0.013 |
| P1 y | 0.322 | 0.049 | 0.012 | 0.362 | 0.050 | 0.015 |
| P4 x | 0.322 | 0.121 | 0.024 | 0.380 | 0.119 | 0.027 |
| P4 y | 0.389 | 0.098 | 0.027 | 0.441 | 0.099 | 0.028 |
| **P1 − P4 x** | **0.155** (0.132–0.185) | **0.119** (0.107–0.133) | **0.026** (0.022–0.031) | **0.156** (0.130–0.195) | **0.116** (0.106–0.128) | **0.030** (0.025–0.036) |
| **P1 − P4 y** | **0.150** (0.118–0.201) | **0.089** (0.081–0.101) | **0.030** (0.025–0.035) | **0.153** (0.121–0.206) | **0.093** (0.084–0.105) | **0.032** (0.027–0.037) |

The floor from the mean spectrum of 250-frame windows is 0.027 / 0.031 px (left x / y)
and 0.032 / 0.034 px (right). The per-window medians read 4–6 % lower. The self-test shows
about 4 % of that is the estimator's own bias (it takes the median of a χ² variable).

**The findings:**

1. **The real P1 − P4 jitter is not white.**
   - The step autocorrelation is +0.15 to +0.20 in x and 0.00 in y, not −0.5.
   - The spectrum has a broad band from about 40 to 160 Hz, peaking at 60–80 Hz.
   - There it reaches 1.6 × 10⁻⁴ px²/Hz (left x), about 55 × the floor of about
     3 × 10⁻⁶ above 200 Hz.
   - It adds 0.075–0.109 px RMS per axis over 30–180 Hz.
   - The step RMS / √2 is 2.1–3.2 × the floor. For white noise it would equal it.
   - So the three measures of "jitter" differ five-fold: floor 0.026–0.032 px, step RMS
     0.089–0.119 px, 200 ms SD 0.150–0.156 px. The SD also includes fixational drift,
     median 2.5–2.6 px/s.
2. **The band is P4's.**
   - P4 alone carries it at the same level.
   - P1 at 70–80 Hz is 33 × lower.
   - P1's own excess sits at 30–50 Hz, the tail of eye and head motion.
3. **It is partly shared between the eyes.**
   - Left–right coherence is 0.22–0.35 at 80–150 Hz, in P4 and in P1 − P4.
   - Above 200 Hz it is 0.00, so the floor is independent per camera, as detector noise
     would be.
4. **It does not fade as a fixation goes on.** Left x step RMS is 0.126 px in a run's first
   100 ms and 0.113 px after 800 ms. A decaying post-saccadic lens oscillation is therefore
   not most of it.
5. **What it is cannot be settled from positions.** Two explanations fit the data:
   - a physiological P4 (lens) motion;
   - an intensity artifact acting through P4's low threshold (T_pup = 13 / 19 here), for
     example illumination flicker seen by both cameras.

   A model-eye recording, which has no physiology, separates the two. Raw video shows it
   directly.
6. **P1's and P4's floors correlate +0.12 to +0.25 frame by frame**, so part of them cancels
   in P1 − P4. The synthetic model makes them independent.
7. **Robust to the two free choices.** Over V = 15–60 px/s and W = 50–250 frames, the
   medians of step RMS and floor move by about 10 % or less. The SD grows with W, because
   of drift. Windows inside completed target periods (1,966 left, 1,968 right) give the
   same medians.

`compare_synthetic.py` inverts §7.1's noise sweep: synthetic P1 − P4 noise SD against P4
SNR, interpolated log-log.

| | Left x | Left y | Right x | Right y |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Real floor, px | 0.027 | 0.031 | 0.032 | 0.034 |
| **P4 SNR at which the spike's code shows that noise** (per-window IQR) | **50** (45–63) | **43** (38–55) | **43** (38–54) | **40** (36–52) |

**How to read the SNR row.** On synthetic frames, the spike's code matches OpenIrisDPI's
real white-noise floor at a **P4 SNR of about 40–50**. That is an equivalent SNR, not a
measured P4 brightness. It rests on two assumptions:

- that OpenIrisDPI's centroid is as efficient as ours. It is the same published algorithm,
  but here with a wider blur and lower thresholds (§7b.1);
- that the floor is detector noise. It is independent between the two cameras, which fits.

**P1 does not fit the same way.**

- The real P1 floor (0.016–0.017 px) is 2.2–2.6 × the model's P1 at base noise.
- The model reaches it at a noise scale of 3.3–3.9, where the model's P4 SNR would be
  20–23. That is half what P4's own floor implies.
- So, against this recording, one of three things holds: the model's P1 is too clean, its
  P4 is too dim relative to P1, or real P1 carries noise the model lacks. The positions
  cannot say which.

**The spike's precision conclusion (§2, §7.1): what the real data supports.**

- P4, not P1, sets P1 − P4's noise. P4 carries almost all of the 40–160 Hz band, and about
  three quarters of the white floor's variance (0.027–0.031 px against P1's 0.016–0.017).
  The positions cannot test the algorithm itself; that needs frames.
- The real floor corresponds to a plausible P4 SNR, between the spike's base case (76) and
  the paper's implied figure. At this recording's own gain it is at or below the paper's
  in-vivo WN-RMS, if the targets are in degrees.

**What it undercuts.**

- The spike treated precision as white noise set by P4's SNR. On this recording, most of
  the fixation jitter is a band-limited P4 component. It makes the step RMS 2.1–3.2 × what
  white noise at the floor would give. The synthetic model has none of it, and no gain in
  P4 SNR would remove it if it is physiological.
- The spike's base case (SNR 76, 0.018 px) is 1.5–1.8 × cleaner than OpenIrisDPI's real
  floor here.
- At this recording's scale, the paper's figure is reached at SNR 28–35, not 24–27.

### 7b.8 What this suggests for the synthetic model (not rerun)

1. **Noise:** base P4 SNR about 45, not 76, to match OpenIrisDPI's real floor. Keep the
   sweep.
2. **A P4-only band-limited displacement:**
   - 0.075–0.11 px RMS per axis over 30–180 Hz, peaking at 60–80 Hz;
   - coherence about 0.3 between the eyes at 80–150 Hz;
   - absent from P1.

   Precision should then be reported by band, not as one number.
3. **Fixational drift:** P1 − P4 drift of 2.5–2.6 px/s median (p95 6.4–7.1 px/s). The
   synthetic positions are static.
4. **P1:** more noise (floor about 0.016 px), or a P1 that is not flatly saturated. P1 and
   P4 frame noise should correlate by about +0.1 to +0.25.
5. **Dropout and blinks:**
   - blinks at about 6.6 per minute, with the pupil lost for a median 38 ms (longest
     108 ms);
   - about 50 ms of disturbed P4 on each side of a blink, and a garbage P4 while the pupil
     is lost;
   - P1 lost at extreme gaze, in runs up to 3.4 s;
   - P4 lost in short runs (median 2–4 ms);
   - whole-pixel P4 runs against the frame edge.
6. **Settings:** rerun the precision bench with the recording's settings before any
   comparison with OpenIrisDPI: blur 4 / 3, T_pup 13 / 19, R_cr 35, R_erode 5.
7. **Geometry:** P1 − P4 at the grid center is (−51, 93) px left and (43, 85) px right,

## 8. What the paper leaves unspecified

These are where a clean-room build can diverge from OpenIrisDPI, each with our choice and
what the spike saw.

1. **Blur** (line 7): R_b's value (not in Fig. 2); how a radius maps to kernel size and σ;
   the border rule; whether the output stays 8-bit. We chose 5 taps, OpenCV's default
   σ = 1.1, replicated borders and 8-bit output. **It is sensitive:** no blur fails at low
   SNR. The tutorial recording's settings give `BlurRadius` 4 / 3 (§7b.1); their semantics
   are unverified.
2. **Crop D** (line 6): its placement, and whether it equals the camera ROI. We used the
   whole 720 × 450 frame.
3. **Laplacian kernel** (line 11). We chose 4-neighbor. An 8-neighbor kernel changes the
   hull on every non-blink frame and moves the pupil center by up to 0.05 px, with no
   effect on P1 or P4.
4. **Hull and ellipse** (lines 13–14): whether the ellipse is fitted to the hull vertices
   or to all boundary points, and by which method (OpenCV has three). We fitted the
   vertices with Halir–Flusser direct least squares. This affects pupil output only.
5. **ExtractROI** (lines 17, 24): square or disc, radius semantics, clipping. We chose a
   square of 2R + 1, clipped.
6. **MaskRegion** (line 19): its shape. We chose a disc of radius R_cr. **It is
   sensitive:** it sets the minimum P1–P4 separation (§7.3).
7. **FillContour + Erode** (lines 20–21): kernel shape and iterations. We used the exact
   geometric inset. R_erode (not in Fig. 2) we set to 8. **It is sensitive:** it sets how
   near the edge P4 can be found. The tutorial recording's `PupilMaskErodeRadius` is 5
   (§7b.1).
8. **ArgMax ties** (line 23). We take the first in raster order.
9. **Comparisons** (< against ≤ at thresholds), and **pixel-coordinate convention** (is a
   pixel center at integer or at +0.5?). A convention offset cancels in P1 − P4 but not in
   pupil or P-CR outputs.
10. **Failure and validity:** no pupil, no CR, no P4, blink, implausible ellipse. **None
    of this is specified.** §7.5 shows it must be designed.
11. **Pixel format:** Mono8 or higher bit depth. T_cr = 245 implies 8-bit, but that is an
    inference.
12. **Line 25's threshold** is T_pup. It is published and was followed, but it ties P4's
    detectability to the pupil setting: P4 must peak above T_pup.
13. **The published algorithm's Algorithm 1 image was not seen.** We used the v2
    preprint's text; the prose around it is identical.
14. **Fig. B.1's caption against its plot:** exp(−r²/6) against /12 (§6.3).
15. **Framework behavior:** frame buffering, threading, timestamps, drop handling and
    output smoothing. The ETRA paper describes a FIFO buffer and per-pipeline threads, but
    OpenIrisDPI's use of them is not in the JNM paper.

## 9. What the spike cannot test (bring-up questions)

1. **Acquisition on Linux at 500 Hz × 2:** Spinnaker/PySpin against Aravis, the license
   ADR already required by P9 §7. 720 × 450 at 500 Hz in 8 bits is 162 MB/s per camera, by
   arithmetic. The camera's frame rate at that ROI is UNVERIFIED here, beyond the paper's
   use of it.
2. **USB behavior:** one host controller per camera (planned in P9); buffer counts;
   dropped frames; per-frame chunk timestamps and frame IDs.
3. **Real eyes:**
   - P4's SNR, size and shape at 940 nm with QE below 10 %, which decides precision (§7.1);
   - tear film, lashes and real blinks;
   - P3;
   - tremor and lens wobble;
   - where P4 is lost against the iris;
   - depth translation, which our model omits. A 1 mm depth change is a 0.18 % scale
     change at 570 mm, which at 5° eccentricity (about 45 px of P1−P4) is 0.08 px.
4. **OS scheduling on the rig:** isolated cores, SCHED_FIFO, frequency governor and IRQ
   affinity. Tails must be measured there; nothing in §7.2 transfers.
5. **Same frames as OpenIrisDPI.** The PI's tutorial bundle has no video: its settings say
   `RecordVideo` false, and its log records 0 raw frames. §7b measured what the bundle
   does hold: OpenIrisDPI's real jitter in pixels, its dropout and blink behavior, and its
   frame timing. §7b.8 lists what that suggests for the synthetic model. The same-frames
   comparison still needs recorded video.
6. **End-to-end latency** from exposure to a gaze sample in `taskd`, against OpenIrisDPI's
   UDP path (S5 §3 and V3).
7. **Sharing the box with P9's 200 fps cameras:** CPU, USB and memory bandwidth together.

## 10. Recommendation

**Build it, on four conditions.** Unchanged by §7b, except that conditions 1 and 2 are
sharpened.

1. **A same-frames validation before any experiment depends on it.** Record raw eye video
   and OpenIrisDPI's own output together; OpenIris can record video (ETRA paper). Run this
   tracker on that video and compare white-noise RMS and the P1 − P4 traces against an
   acceptance criterion the PI sets. §7b adds three requirements:
   - **Run with OpenIrisDPI's own settings from that session**: blur, erosion and
     thresholds. The tutorial recording's differ from the spike's (§7b.1).
   - **Compare spectra, not one number.** On the tutorial recording, OpenIrisDPI's
     P1 − P4 has a P4-only 40–160 Hz component. It makes the sample-to-sample RMS
     2.1–3.2 × what white noise at the floor would give (§7b.5).
   - **Include a model-eye recording.** A model eye has no physiology, so it tells whether
     that component is the eye or the tracker.
2. **Validity logic designed in from the start**, reporting invalid frames as missing
   (S5's staleness path) and never as a best guess. Blinks are the case (§7.5).
   **OpenIrisDPI cannot be the reference for validity.** On its tutorial recording:
   - `DataQuality` is 100 on every frame;
   - it reports a P4 through every blink;
   - the 50 ms either side of each blink carry unflagged P4 jumps (§7b.2–§7b.3).

   Score validity against the frames themselves.
3. **Edge behavior decided.** Default R_P4 small (about 6 px in this model) and flag P4
   near the hull boundary, or keep the published R_P4 = 15 to match OpenIrisDPI. Decide
   only after condition 1 shows what OpenIrisDPI does on real eyes.
4. **500 Hz × 2 acquisition proven on the rig's Linux** with the chosen camera library, and
   tails measured there with real-time tuning.

**Core language: C++.** The reasons:

- 1.4–1.7 × less CPU than numba here, on identical outputs;
- no JIT at service start, and no LLVM runtime in the hot path;
- runnable as its own process pinned to an isolated core, which is where Linux tail
  control lives;
- 421 lines, small enough to review statement for statement against the numpy reference,
  which becomes the test oracle, with no numba needed.

The cost is the first compiled component in a pure-Python repo: a build step, a compiler
on the build host, and an ADR under the dependency policy. That is the PI's call (§11,
item 3). numba is a workable fallback: it met the budget at 720 × 450 in both runs, but
its median was less stable under load.

**Effort — an ESTIMATE, not measured.** It assumes P9's camera framework and the
camera-library ADR are done first.

## 11. The PI's questions

1. **Same-frames validation source.** Is it acceptable to require raw eye video recorded
   alongside OpenIrisDPI's output before this tracker is used in any experiment? Where
   would that video come from: our rig once the cameras arrive, or a collaborator's
   OpenIrisDPI rig?
2. **Acceptance criterion.** How close to OpenIrisDPI on the same frames is close enough to
   switch? For example, white-noise RMS within 10 % of theirs, and no systematic P1 − P4
   offset above some number of pixels.
3. **A compiled core.** Is a small C++ library, built with the package and needing a
   compiler at build time and an ADR, acceptable? Or should the tracker stay pure Python
   with numba (1.4–1.7 × more CPU here, a JIT, and a large LLVM dependency)?
4. **Invalid frames.** When the tracker cannot vouch for P4 (a blink, P4 lost, an
   implausible pupil), should expcontroller receive "no sample" so that S5's staleness
   rules apply, rather than a best estimate?
5. **Transition.** A USB camera can be opened by only one process. Should the lab keep
   OpenIrisDPI on its own PC and cameras through validation, or validate offline from
   recorded video only?

### The PI's answers (2026-09-27, asked in plain terms)

1. **Validation source: our rig, once the cameras arrive.** Raw eye video is recorded
   alongside OpenIrisDPI there, and the new tracker is compared on those same frames
   before it is used in any experiment.
2. **Acceptance: match or beat OpenIrisDPI on everything** — at least as precise, and no
   systematic P1 − P4 offset — before switching.
3. **A C++ core is acceptable**, with its ADR (a compiler at build time, the license and
   dependency entry).
4. **Invalid frames reach expcontroller as "no sample"**, so S5's staleness rules apply,
   never a best estimate.
5. **Transition: OpenIrisDPI stays live on its own PC and cameras; the new tracker is
   validated offline, from recorded video only**, until it passes.
