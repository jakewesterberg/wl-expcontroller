## 1. Answer

1. **Direct viewing is not designed anywhere yet** (§2). S0 §5, S4 §2, architecture.md
   ("Stereo, as viewports") and the controller architecture's D6 all route every task
   through the split-screen stereoscope ("the monocular v1 task is the zero-disparity case
   of the same path"). The one place the stereoscope leaves the path is an open question
   about touch (controller architecture §8.4). The following have no spec for direct view:
   - photodiode patch placement;
   - the display module's viewport mapping;
   - the `Geometry` class;
   - V9.
2. **At a fixed distance, the bigger panel moves the eye camera only about 2° further off
   axis** (§4).
   - The camera must look past the screen's bottom edge, and its IR LED has to sit about 10°
     shallower than the camera to center P4, as the paper's does.
   - At 57 cm that puts the lowest usable camera angle at **31.9° for the 32-inch and 29.9°
     for the 27-inch**.
   - **Both are inside the paper's 35°.** The paper's own layout, camera at 35° and LED at
     25°, fits under either screen unchanged.
3. **At any given camera angle, the 32-inch shows at least as much field as the 27-inch.**
   - Both angles are set by how far the screen's lower edge sits below the eye, and the
     27-inch has twice the housing border (3.6 cm against 1.8 cm).
   - Examples: 32-inch at 57 cm against 27-inch at 50 cm; 32-inch at 65 cm against 27-inch
     at 57 cm (§4.2).
   - **So choose the distance, not the panel.**
4. **The real limit at 15° is the eye, not the camera angle, and it is the same on both
   panels** (§6).
   - The paper reports, for real macaques in this configuration: "P4 was visible for eye
     positions up to 10° from central position in any direction before becoming obscured by
     the iris".
   - In the synthetic model anchored to that report, moving the camera anywhere from 0° to
     45° shifts the reach by about 1° at the paper's pupil, and about 2° at a pupil 1.5×
     larger.
   - **The pupil diameter moves the reach in proportion.** The paper's pupil (about 3.4 mm at
     the spike's ASSUMED magnification) reaches about 10°; about 1.5× that diameter reaches
     15°.
5. **Hot-mirror verdict: not worth it for the camera-angle problem** (§5).
   - It removes the camera angle from the question.
   - It puts a coated glass plate a few centimeters from the animal's face, **12–22 cm wide**
     to cover the whole screen, seen at 26–64° incidence. That costs up to 15% of the
     luminance and brings angle-dependent color shifts, reflections and cleaning.
   - **It changes P4's reach by under 1° in the model.**
   - Keep it in reserve only for a reason other than the camera angle.
6. **The stereoscope's ±10° is met by both panels at 57 cm** (§9): ±17.1° on the 32-inch and
   ±14.5° on the 27-inch.
7. **A 27-inch 4K 240 Hz tandem QD-OLED exists.** It is the ASUS ROG Swift OLED
   **PG27UCDM**: "27-inch (26.5-inch viewable) 4K Tandem QD-OLED", "DisplayPort™ 2.1a UHBR20
   (80Gbps full bandwidth)", "4K at 240Hz ... without compression", three-year warranty
   including burn-in, $1,099.00 (rog.asus.com, 2026-09-27). The MSI MPG 272URX QD-OLED is a
   second (§8).
8. **Recommendation: keep the 32-inch and view it directly at 57 cm**, or at up to 65 cm if a
   lower camera is wanted (§10). The 15° reach is a tracker question for bring-up, not a
   reason to change the panel.

## 2. Direct viewing is not yet designed

What exists assumes the stereoscope:

- **S0 §5.2 and the optics drawing:** every number is per-eye, through mirrors.
- **S4 §2:** "the display module maps them to per-eye viewport pixels using measured
  optics", and "A monocular task is the zero-disparity case of the same path".
- **S4 §7:** the photodiode patches live in "the bottom strip ... created by stopping the far
  mirror to ±17° vertical ... Outside both viewports, so neither is visible to either eye."
- **architecture.md:** "Split-screen mirror stereoscope on **one panel** ... The monocular v1
  task is the zero-disparity case of the same path."
- **Controller architecture §8.4:** "Open item: whether touch is a second panel, a rig mode
  with the stereoscope out of path, or deferred."
- **`wl_expcontroller/geometry.py`:** it models a viewport half the panel wide.

What direct view needs that nothing specifies:

1. **A viewport mapping for one full-panel view** at a measured eye-to-screen distance, with
   no folded path.
2. **Photodiode patches.** With no mirrors, any patch on the panel is in the animal's view.
   The flip patch alternates every refresh (S4 §7), so it would be a flickering distractor at
   the screen's corner. A direct-view rig needs a physical shield over the patch with the
   sensor under it, or a patch location the animal cannot see. That is S0 open item 5 again,
   in a new geometry.
3. **V9 and V1** as written test per-half photometry and per-eye paths. Direct view needs its
   own acceptance items. ABL across the whole panel still matters, now as a within-image
   nonlinearity rather than interocular coupling.
4. **How a rig switches between direct view and the stereoscope,** and how the session
   snapshot records which one ran.

This report does not design any of that. It is listed so the PI can see it is open.

## 3. Direct-view geometry

**Assumptions:**

- The eye is on the perpendicular through the center of the active area.
- Panels use the makers' active areas: PG32UCDM3 699.48 × 394.73 mm, PG27UCDM
  589.97 × 332.93 mm.
- Housing borders are split evenly top and bottom (ASSUMED; §4.2 gives the bound with all of
  it at the bottom).

The relations:

- field = `atan(W/2 / D)` × `atan(H/2 / D)`;
- px/deg at eccentricity `e` = `D / (pitch · cos² e)` in pixels per radian;
- the 15° margin is the vertical half-field minus 15°, because vertical is the tighter
  dimension on a 16:9 panel.

Worked example, 32-inch at 57 cm:

- horizontal: `atan(34.97 / 57) = 31.5°`;
- vertical: `atan(19.74 / 57) = 19.1°`;
- the housing is 41.3 cm tall, so the border is (41.3 − 39.47) / 2 = 0.91 cm, and the
  housing edge is at `atan(20.65 / 57) = 19.9°`;
- the pitch is 69.948 / 3840 = 0.01822 cm, so `57 / 0.01822` px per radian is 54.6 px/deg
  at the center.

The side edges fall at ±31.5° (32-inch) and ±27.4° (27-inch) at 57 cm. Including the
housing, they are ±32.2° and ±28.2°.

**Reading the table:**

- **The 27-inch cannot show 15° at 65 cm or beyond, and the 32-inch cannot at 75 cm.**
- At 57 cm, the 27-inch leaves 1.3° beyond a 15° target. A fixation window or a stimulus
  larger than that would be clipped. The 32-inch leaves 4.1°.
- Direct view has no folded path, so `D` is the physical distance and the accommodation
  demand is `1/D`.

## 4. The eye camera in direct view (baseline: below the screen)

### 4.1 What sets the angle

The camera and its IR LED must see the eye past the screen's bottom edge, with a clearance
margin.

- **Margin:** 2° (ASSUMED). That is the 1.8° half-angle of a 100 mm f/2.8 lens's 35.7 mm
  entrance pupil seen from 57 cm, plus a little.
- **The 10° rule:** the paper's rule is that "the infrared illuminator is placed at a
  shallower angle than the imaging cameras", which "centers P4 in the pupil for neutral
  gaze" (Ressmeyer et al. 2026, §3.1). Its values are LED at 25° and camera at 35°. Taking
  that 10° offset as transferable (ASSUMED) gives:

```
LED floor     = housing bottom-edge angle + 2 deg
camera (P4)   = LED floor + 10 deg        (the lowest camera that still centers P4)
```

A camera alone, with no P4 centering, needs only the LED floor. That is 17–27° across the
table.

### 4.2 The angles, for both panels and all distances

| D | 32: bottom edge (housing) | 32: lowest P4-centering camera | 27: bottom edge (housing) | 27: lowest P4-centering camera | Paper's 35°/25° fits? (32 / 27) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 45 cm | 24.6° | **36.6°** | 22.3° | 34.3° | no / yes |
| 50 cm | 22.4° | 34.4° | 20.3° | 32.3° | yes / yes |
| **57 cm** | **19.9°** | **31.9°** | **17.9°** | **29.9°** | **yes / yes** |
| 65 cm | 17.6° | 29.6° | 15.9° | 27.9° | yes / yes |
| 75 cm | 15.4° | 27.4° | 13.8° | 25.8° | yes / yes |

**With all of the housing border at the bottom** (the bound; `--border-bottom-all`):

- the 32-inch angles rise by about 1°, to 32.7° at 57 cm;
- the 27-inch angles rise by about 2°, to 31.6° at 57 cm;
- the paper's layout then fails for the 32-inch at 50 cm and for the 27-inch at 45 cm.

**The difference between panels at a fixed distance is 1–2°.** The 27-inch's advantage is
mostly spent on its thicker border. Pairs matched by camera angle:

- **32-inch at 57 cm** (31.9°, 15° margin +4.1°) against **27-inch at 50 cm** (32.3°, +3.4°);
- **32-inch at 65 cm** (29.6°, +1.9°) against **27-inch at 57 cm** (29.9°, +1.3°).

In both pairs the 32-inch shows more field at a slightly lower camera angle.

**Against the paper's own rig.** Its monkeys sat 61 cm from a rear-projected image 51 cm
wide. Taking the image as 16:9 from its 1280 × 720 grid (ASSUMED), the lower edge was at
`atan(14.3 / 61) = 13.2°`. The camera sat 22° below that and the LED 12° below it. At 57 cm
the LED's margin below our housing edge is:

- 32-inch: 25 − 19.9 = **5.1°**;
- 27-inch: 7.1°.

Both are positive and both are smaller than the paper's. How much LED clearance a real mount
needs is a bring-up check.

**What the angle does to the signal, in the spike's model.** Vertical P1−P4 motion is
foreshortened by `cos(camera angle)`, so elevation noise in arcmin scales as `1/cos`:

| Camera angle | 1/cos |
|---|---|
| 29.9° (27-inch at 57 cm) | 1.15 |
| 31.9° (32-inch at 57 cm) | 1.18 |
| 35° (paper) | 1.22 |

That is a **2% difference between the panels**. The paper does see azimuth noise below
elevation noise and suggests the oblique geometry as the cause. P4's reach against camera
angle is in §6.

**Where the camera sits.** At the paper's 57 cm working distance and 35°, the camera is
46.7 cm forward of the eyes and 32.7 cm below them. With the screen at 57 cm, that is in
front of the screen plane and 12 cm below the 32-inch housing. From the eye, the top of an
ASSUMED Ø75 mm lens is at about 31° below the line of sight, well clear of the image edge at
19.1°. The same holds for the 27-inch. **Nothing in the geometry favors either panel here.**
The muzzle, the juice spout and the chair front along that line are bring-up checks, and they
are identical for both.

## 5. The alternative: a hot mirror

A hot mirror is a dichroic plate at 45° in front of the eyes. It passes visible light and
reflects the 940 nm IR to a camera above, below or beside. It makes the camera angle
independent of the screen, which is why most earlier DPI trackers used one. The OpenIrisDPI
paper, §3.1: "Most previously described DPI eye trackers use a hot mirror to align the
optical axes of the eye and camera ... This approach minimizes signal non-linearity ...
However, a hot mirror also adds complexity by filtering the light between the display and the
subject." Wu et al. 2023's dDPI used "a custom short-pass dichroic mirror that selectively
reflected infrared light at a 45° angle while letting through visible light", with the
camera "physically placed laterally to the eye, at a 90° angle".

### 5.1 Size and placement

The plate must cover both eyes' lines of sight to every part of the screen the animal uses;
otherwise its edge is a visible step across the image. A 45° plate tilted about a
horizontal axis, `d` cm in front of the eyes, meets rays at `d/(1 ± tan θ_V)`, so it is a
trapezoid (`direct_view.py`, `hot_mirror`):

| Coverage at 57 cm | d = 6 cm | d = 8 cm | d = 10 cm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 32-inch, whole screen | 14.5 (far edge) × 6.7 cm | 18.2 × 8.9 cm | 22.0 × 11.1 cm |
| 27-inch, whole screen | 12.0 × 5.4 cm | 14.9 × 7.2 cm | 17.8 × 9.0 cm |
| ±17° only (either panel; the mirror becomes the field stop) | 8.5 × 5.7 cm | 10.2 × 7.6 cm | 12.0 × 9.5 cm |

Width is at the far edge; length is along the tilt; `IPD` is 3.2 cm.

Edmund Optics stocks a 45° hot mirror at **101 × 127 mm** (#64-472):

- BOROFLOAT, 3.30 mm thick;
- "Tavg >85% @ 400 - 690nm, Ravg >95% @ 750 - 1125nm";
- back face "AR: Ravg <1% @ 400 - 700nm";
- flatness 4–6λ P-V;
- $239.00.

It also offers "Quote Your Size" (edmundoptics.com, read 2026-09-27). Thorlabs' soda-lime hot
mirrors top out at Ø2″ and 25 × 36 mm, and its 45° version is a "Red Hot Mirror" that
reflects 610–725 nm, which would remove the display's red (thorlabs.com, read 2026-09-27).

**So:**

- a stock 127 mm plate covers the whole 27-inch only within about 6 cm of the eyes, and the
  whole 32-inch only within about 5 cm;
- at a more practical 8–10 cm, full coverage needs a custom size;
- alternatively the plate is also the field stop at about ±17°, and the rest of the panel is
  wasted.

The camera goes below, above or beside the plate.

- Below, its path runs down past the chin, the chest and the chair front.
- Above, it passes the head-post hardware.

At the paper's 57 cm working distance, the camera sits about half a meter from the plate,
which is a build question. A shorter lens at the same magnification shortens that path: by
thin-lens arithmetic, about 50 mm at 28 cm.

### 5.2 What it costs

1. **Luminance.** The stock part guarantees only Tavg > 85% across the visible, so up to 15%
   of the display's light is lost. S0 §5.4 already makes sustained full-field luminance the
   binding figure for panel life.
2. **Color and uniformity, by angle.** Edmund Optics' application note on filter
   orientation (read 2026-09-27) says: "Changing AOI outside a filter's specified range will
   shift the peak transmission towards shorter wavelengths ... increasing AOI directly impacts
   transmission efficiency ... At more extreme angles ... the entire spectral shape deforms".
   - A 45° plate in front of the 32-inch at 57 cm sees incidence from 26° to 64° down the
     screen's height, and 53° at the sides (§5.1 script). The 27-inch spans 29° to 61°.
   - **Luminance and color will vary across the image in a way calibration through the
     plate must measure.** V9's photometry would have to be done through the mirror, at
     several field positions.
   - How far the red edge moves on these parts is not published: **UNVERIFIED**.
3. **Reflections.** A dichroic that transmits "> 85%" reflects part of the rest. The animal
   sees a faint reflection of whatever faces the coated side, such as the camera, the chair or
   its own lit face, laid over the stimulus. Everything there must be matte black. The stock
   part's AR back face limits the second-surface ghost; its level at 26–64° is not stated
   (UNVERIFIED).
4. **Geometry.** A 3.3 mm plate shifts the image sideways by `t · sin(θ − θr) / cos θr`,
   which is 0.5 mm at 26° and 1.9 mm at 64° (`n` = 1.47 ASSUMED). That is a smooth
   distortion of about 0.13° across the field, which gaze calibration absorbs. It is not
   zero.
5. **Cleaning and safety.** A coated glass surface 6–10 cm from a macaque's face will collect
   condensation, saliva and juice, and needs routine cleaning. It must be guarded against
   breakage near the eyes. The makers' pages read here give no cleaning specification
   (UNVERIFIED).
6. **P4 reach.** In the model, putting the camera on axis changes P4's reach by under 1°, in
   either direction depending on the foreshortening assumed (§6.2). It makes the reach
   symmetric, not larger.

**Verdict:** for the camera-angle problem at 57 cm, **no**. The direct-below-screen baseline
already fits the paper's geometry on either panel, and the mirror's costs land on the visual
stimulus, which is the thing the rig exists to control. It remains the option if a future
rig must put the camera nearer the line of sight, or place the screen closer than about
50 cm with the 32-inch.

## 6. P4 at 15°: the synthetic check (the same for both panels)

**SYNTHETIC. These are not rig or eye numbers.** `p4_range.py` reuses the spike's frame model
(`synth.py`) and its detector (`dpi_numba.py`, the paper's Algorithm 1) unchanged. It adds
two things a wide grid needs:

- the camera ROI follows the pupil;
- P4 is occluded by the iris, which the spike's renderer did not model.

The gaze grid is ±17° in 1° steps (1,225 positions, 3 noise frames each). Each point is
classified:

- **lost:** P4 found in fewer than 2 of 3 frames;
- **wrong:** found, with any error above 2 px;
- **biased:** found, with a mean error above 0.1 px;
- **ok:** otherwise.

The per-point maps for every condition are in `p4_range.json` under `class_maps`.

### 6.1 Two models, and why the second is the one to read

- **"Committed"** is the spike's geometry as written. P4 moves 3.4 px/deg relative to the
  pupil, a human-eye figure (Wu et al. 2023) combined with an ASSUMED pupil lever arm. It
  keeps P4 inside the pupil almost to ±17°, which **contradicts the paper's real-eye 10°**.
  It also puts the P1/P4 crossing at about 11° down-left, as an artifact of combining FIG2's
  layout with a human gain. Treat it as the optimistic bound and nothing more.
- **"Anchored"** takes the paper at its word:
  - P4 centered at neutral gaze (paper §3.1);
  - P4's travel relative to the pupil solved so that it first reaches the apparent pupil
    edge 10° from center (paper §2.2). That is **10.5 px/deg, about 0.17 mm/deg** at the spike's
    ASSUMED 16.2 µm/px, three times the committed figure;
  - pupil foreshortening fitted to FIG2's own 97/105 aspect (ASSUMED);
  - P1 placed per Wu 2023, so that it overlaps P4 only when the eye points at the LED.

  The anchored model reproduces the paper's 10° by construction. What it adds is how the
  reach scales with pupil size and camera angle, and what the detector does near the edge.

### 6.2 Results: anchored model, camera at 35° (the paper's)

Pupil sizes are scaled from FIG2's pupil (3.4 mm at the spike's ASSUMED magnification).
Macaque pupil diameters in dim light were not found in a primary source this session, so the
sweep is **ASSUMED** to span the plausible range.

Read the table this way:

- **At the paper's pupil, P4 is lost over about half of the 15° disc on either panel.**
- The reach scales with pupil diameter. About 1.5× the paper's pupil reaches 15° horizontally
  and further vertically.
- **"Wrong" is the published R_P4 = 15 pulling P4 toward the iris by up to 12 px** in the
  last 1–2° before loss, the spike's §7.4 finding. At the spike's conversion (6.76′/px,
  human gain) that is about 80′, reported as a confident sample.
- **R_P4 = 6 removes it here too.** The worst mean error inside 15° drops from 12 px to
  0.2–0.4 px.

**Camera angle barely matters.** The reach below is the minimum over gaze directions, from
the anchored model; the detector column is the grid run with R_P4 = 6.

| Camera | Reach, FIG2 foreshortening (1.0× / 1.5× pupil) | Reach, plain cosine, pessimistic (1.0× / 1.5×) | Detector, disc ≤ 15° ok (1.0× / 1.5×) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0° (hot mirror) | 10.0° / 15.0° | 10.7° / 15.8° | 39% / 93% |
| 30° | 10.0° / 15.0° | 10.1° / 14.6° | 43% / 97% |
| 35° (paper) | 10.0° / 15.0° | 10.0° / 14.3° | 44% / 98% |
| 40° | 10.0° / 15.0° | 9.9° / 14.0° | 46% / 99% |
| 45° | 10.0° / 15.0° | 9.7° / 13.7° | — |

Why the columns disagree:

- **With FIG2's foreshortening,** the tightest direction is horizontal, which the camera's
  elevation does not touch. A steeper camera compresses P4's vertical travel a little more
  than it narrows the pupil, so the grid's ok-fraction rises slightly with the angle.
- **With plain cosine foreshortening** (no corneal refraction), the vertical direction binds
  and the reach falls by about 0.2–0.3° per 5° of camera angle.
- **Either way, 0–45° spans about 1° at the paper's pupil and about 2° at 1.5×.**

The panel changes the camera angle by 1–2° (§4.2). By this table that changes P4's reach by
a tenth of a degree or less. **The panel does not decide whether 15° is trackable. The pupil
does.**

### 6.3 What would extend the reach

In rough order of cost:

1. **A larger pupil: a dimmer mean background.** In the model this is the strongest lever,
   and it costs nothing in hardware. The price is photometric: the stimulus background sets
   the pupil. This is a science-facing trade and a question for the PI, not a default.
2. **R_P4 = 6 and validity flags near the pupil edge.** This does not extend the reach. It
   turns the last 1–2° from confidently wrong into correct or missing, which is what S5's
   staleness path needs (P10 condition 3).
3. **P-CR at the edges.** OpenIrisDPI already outputs the pupil and CR, and the paper
   proposes "a hybrid system that dynamically switches between methods, leveraging DPI for
   high-precision fixational measurements and P-CR for lower-precision estimates of larger
   gaze angles".
   - P-CR keeps working where P4 is hidden, at about three times the white noise the paper
     reports, and with pupil-decentration drift.
   - **It collides with a contract.** `wl-preproc` refuses any `raw_definition` other than
     `"CR1 - CR4"` (S5, the published map format). A P-CR map would need an amendment there,
     or would have to stay inside expcontroller as an online-only map.
4. **More illuminators.** The paper's suggestion: each LED centers P4 at a different gaze. In
   the model, at the paper's pupil:
   - four LEDs at (±6.5°, ±6.5°) cover **99%** of the 15° disc;
   - one LED covers **42%**;
   - two LEDs at ±7° cover 74%.

   The cost is several P1/P4 pairs per frame, or LEDs switched frame by frame. That is a
   change to the published algorithm, whose validation is P10's condition 1, and more IR
   dose to measure.
5. **A second camera** (also the paper's suggestion): the same idea on the imaging side, at
   twice the camera budget per eye.
6. **A hot mirror: no meaningful gain in reach** (table above; under 1° either way). Its case
   is geometric (§5), not P4.

**The fixed 500 Hz ROI is a separate limit.** The paper reached 500 Hz by cropping to
720 × 450. At the spike's ASSUMED magnification and pupil travel (10.3 mm lever arm, human),
the pupil's edge leaves a fixed 720 × 450 window at:

| Pupil | Up | Down |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0× | 15.0° | 13.6° |
| 1.5× | 9.6° | 8.3° |

So **the larger pupil that P4 needs does not fit the paper's 500 Hz ROI at ±15°** unless the
ROI follows the eye or the magnification drops. A lower magnification costs precision
proportionally. Whether the camera can move its ROI per frame is UNVERIFIED.

### 6.4 What this cannot say

- The anchored gain rests on one sentence and one screenshot. The FIG2 layout cannot be
  reconciled with human DPI gains at neutral gaze: it shows an 88 px P1–P4 separation where
  the human figure predicts about 200 px for an LED 25° off axis. Either the screenshot was
  not at neutral gaze, or the macaque gains are much smaller than the human ones. If it is
  the latter, the spike's arcminute conversions (P10 §5.2) are optimistic by about 2×.
  **Only a real recording settles it.**
- The model has no tear film, lid, lashes or lens wobble.
- The model has no corneal-refraction model of the pupil beyond the FIG2 fit.

## 7. What the viewing distance costs (direct view)

**Optics.**

- Accommodation demand is `1/D`: 2.22 D at 45 cm, 2.00 D at 50, 1.75 D at 57, 1.54 D at 65
  and 1.33 D at 75.
- In direct view both eyes see one image on the screen, so vergence and accommodation agree
  at `1/D`. There is no conflict and no software offset, unlike the stereoscope (drawing §6).
- In the stereoscope, the vergence-accommodation conflict for a stereo stimulus does not
  depend on `D`. Vergence distance in diopters is about `V / IPD`, with `V` the vergence
  angle in radians, so a disparity δ shifts it by δ/IPD. That is 0.55 D per degree at a 32 mm IPD, whatever `D` is
  (small-angle arithmetic, not a citation).
- Why the conflict matters: Hoffman, Girshick, Akeley & Banks (2008), working with human
  observers, write that "the uncoupling of vergence and accommodation required by 3D displays frequently
  reduces one's ability to fuse the binocular stimulus and causes discomfort and fatigue"
  (J Vis 8(3):33, [doi:10.1167/8.3.33](https://doi.org/10.1167/8.3.33), abstract via PubMed,
  2026-09-27).
- Bito et al. (1982) report the drug-induced accommodative amplitude of rhesus monkeys as a
  mean of 34 D at 1–5 years, falling to about 5 D beyond 25 years (IOVS 23:23–31, PMID
  7085219, abstract via PubMed, 2026-09-27). **Every distance in the table is well inside
  that range for animals of lab age.** How accurately alert macaques accommodate to a 1.3–2.2
  D target is UNVERIFIED.

**Chair and head-post fit.** In direct view the panel's face is at `D`, with nothing between.
Closer is more intrusive and farther needs a bigger enclosure. This is S0 open item 6, now
asked for direct view, and it remains a build measurement.

**The literature's 57 cm.** At 57.3 cm, 1 cm is 1°. S4 works in degrees, so no code
depends on it. The OpenIrisDPI monkeys sat at 61 cm, so the convention is not universal.

**px/deg.** At 57 cm: 54.6 at the center on the 32-inch and 64.8 on the 27-inch. Matched
by camera angle (§4.2), the pairs are close: 32 @ 57 = 54.6 against 27 @ 50 = 56.8, and
32 @ 65 = 62.3 against 27 @ 57 = 64.8.

**Photodiode patches.** No direct-view design exists (§2). Whatever shield covers the patch
sits at the screen's corner. At 57 cm the 32-inch's corner is at about (31.5°, −19.1°) and
the 27-inch's at about (27.4°, −16.3°). Either way the shield is outside the 15° region and
inside the animal's view.

**S4, S5 and the code.** S4's degree-based API carries over. S5's calibration
constellation is defined as 75% of the per-eye field
(`2026-09-05-calibration-constellation.md` §7). That field is the stereoscope's, so it has
no direct-view definition yet.

- If direct-view targets reach 15°, the constellation's outer points sit beyond the paper's
  ~10° P4 reach. **That is already true of the stereoscope constellation**: its edge points
  are at 10.8° and 12.1° and its corners at 16.25°.
- **A calibration that places targets where P4 is lost fits noise.** The acceptance clause
  `constellation-inside-tested-region` sees reach but not trackability.

## 9. The stereoscope (secondary: ±10°)

**Both panels meet ±10° at 57 cm through the drawn stereoscope:** ±17.06° × ±19.10° on the
32-inch and ±14.51° × ±16.28° on the 27-inch (`geometry.py`). The 27-inch would also
work at 48 cm with a field identical to the 32-inch's. No reason to change the panel comes
from the stereoscope.

The four stereoscope options, from `geometry.py` using the drawing's own relations (`E` =
1.6 cm, symmetric rule, vertical stop at ±θ_H):

| | 32 @ 57 | 27 @ 57 | 27 @ 48.1 (same field) | 27 @ 51.4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Field per eye | ±17.06° × ±19.10° | ±14.51° × ±16.28° | ±17.06° × ±19.10° | ±16.00° × ±17.93° |
| px/deg, center (S0 mean) | 54.6 (56.3) | 64.8 (66.2) | 54.6 (56.3) | 58.4 (60.0) |
| Lateral shift `HW − E` | 15.89 cm | 13.15 cm | 13.15 cm | 13.15 cm |
| Physical eye-to-screen distance | 41.11 cm | 43.85 cm | 34.93 cm | 38.29 cm |
| M1 at `a = E / tan θ_H`; size | 5.22 cm; 45 × 32 mm | 6.18 cm; 45 × 32 mm | 5.22 cm; 45 × 32 mm | 5.58 cm; 45 × 32 mm |
| M2 size (drawing relation) | 183 × 129 mm | 141 × 100 mm | 159 × 113 mm | 152 × 107 mm |
| Photodiode strip | 2.25 cm (123 px) | 1.90 cm (123 px) | 1.90 cm (123 px) | 1.90 cm (123 px) |

**The folded path is `HW − E`, not a fixed 15.8 cm.** The mirrors buy less path on the
smaller panel.

**The PI's arithmetic checks out for a 27.0-inch viewable diagonal:** `0.2179 × 68.58 =
14.94 cm`, and `14.94 / tan 17° = 48.88 cm`. The panels on sale are 26.5-inch viewable, with a
589.97 mm active width. Matching the PG32UCDM3's published field therefore takes 48.1 cm,
and exactly ±17.0° takes 48.2 cm.

Around the eyes, the near mirror M1 fills exactly the field cone (`a = E / tan θ_H`), so
the camera's off-axis floor there depends on the field, not the panel. The paper's 35°
camera clears M1 plus a 5 mm mount by about 11°. Its 25° LED clears it by about 3° with the
±17° M1 stop and by about 1° without. For a ±10° requirement the field could be stopped
smaller still, which frees more room.

Recomputing the drawing's mirrors from its own relations (details in `geometry.json`)
found simplifications that matter for **the build** rather than for the panel choice. They
are recorded for the drawing's owner, not edited:

1. **The nasal clip.** The drawing uses `atan(E/a)`. A 45° roof whose ridge is on the
   midline clips at `atan(E/(a − E))`. The symmetric position is therefore about 6.8 cm,
   not 5.2 cm. M1 can sit about 1.6 cm further from the face, which helps with the muzzle
   (drawing open item 4).
2. **The mirror sizes.** The size relation ignores the 45° footprint's trapezoid, which is
   1.44× taller at its temporal edge. On the 32-inch, the exact sizes are:
   - M1: 50 × 46 mm, against the drawn 45 × 32;
   - M2: 202 × 187 mm, against the drawn 183 × 129.

   **Rectangles cut to the drawn sizes would vignette the field's temporal corners.** A
   straight stop edge on a 45° M2 also stops at a sloped line on the screen. A baffle near
   the panel makes a cleaner photodiode strip.
3. §3's M2 center, "x = ∓18.31 cm", contradicts its own 1.60 + 15.83 = 17.43.
4. §3's "mirror plane 7.0 cm / 34.17 cm" is the drawing's state before the symmetric-field
   ruling.
5. S0 §5.2's "1.2 arcmin/pixel" is 1.1′ at 57 cm (1.2′ is the 50 cm figure).

## 10. Recommendation

**Keep the 32-inch PG32UCDM3. For direct viewing, put the eyes at 57 cm, or at up to 65 cm
if a lower camera is wanted.** These pairs meet both constraints:

| | Shows 15° (margin) | Lowest P4-centering camera | Paper's 35°/25° fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **32-inch at 57 cm** (recommended) | yes (+4.1°) | 31.9° | yes |
| 32-inch at 65 cm | yes (+1.9°) | 29.6° | yes |
| 27-inch at 50 cm | yes (+3.4°) | 32.3° | yes |
| 27-inch at 57 cm | yes (+1.3°) | 29.9° | yes |

**Avoid:**

- the 32-inch closer than about 50 cm, where the camera goes past 35°;
- the 27-inch at 65 cm or more, where it cannot show 15°;
- the 32-inch at 75 cm, for the same reason.

**The reasons that decide it:**

1. **The camera angle is set by the distance, not the panel.** At a fixed distance the 32-inch
   puts the camera 1–2° further off axis. At a matched camera angle it shows more field than the
   27-inch, whose thicker border spends most of its advantage. At 57 cm both fit the paper's
   own 35°/25° layout unchanged.
2. **The 15° reach is limited by the eye, identically on both panels.** In the anchored model
   the camera angle moves P4's reach by 1–2° across 0–45°, while the pupil moves it in
   proportion. Swapping the panel does not buy trackability at 15°. Pupil size, R_P4 and
   validity, and a P-CR fallback do.
3. **The 32-inch is already chosen and is the stronger panel.** It is tandem, DisplayHDR 500,
   and carries 4.1° of margin beyond a 15° target at 57 cm, against 1.3° for the 27-inch.
   The stereoscope's ±10° is met by both.

**The hot mirror is not recommended** for this problem (§5).

## 11. What remains a bring-up measurement

1. **P4's real reach and the animals' real pupil diameters** under the rig's actual
   background luminance. Record raw eye video across a 15° target set with the paper's
   35°/25° geometry. This is the measurement that decides whether 15° is DPI-trackable, and
   it is P10's condition 1 recording, extended outward.
2. **The camera and LED clearance under the chosen screen and distance.** That covers the
   housing edge (§4.2 assumes an even border split), the muzzle, the juice spout and the
   chair front along the 35° line, and the LED optic's real size.
3. **Whether the ROI can follow the eye at 500 Hz,** or what magnification ±15° needs (§6.3).
4. **The viewing distance** against the real chair and head-post (S0 open item 6), now for
   direct view.
5. **Pixel shift and proximity-sensor defeat** on the purchased panel (S0 §5.4 test 1). It
   is the same question on either ASUS panel.
6. If a hot mirror is ever used: **luminance and chromaticity through it at several field
   positions** (V9), and its reflections, dark-checked from the animal's position.

## 12. Decisions this raises for the PI (to be asked, not filed)

1. **Direct view needs its own design pass:** viewport mapping, photodiode shields, V9 items,
   and switching between direct view and the stereoscope. Should that be scheduled as a work
   package?
2. **How to hold 15° when P4 is lost.** The options:
   - trade background luminance for pupil size;
   - accept P-CR beyond the DPI reach, which needs a `wl-preproc` amendment to publish;
   - add illuminators;
   - keep fixation targets inside the P4 reach and put only stimuli beyond it.
3. **Whether the stereoscope drawing should be corrected now** (§9) or at the optics build.
