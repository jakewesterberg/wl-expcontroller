# Calibration constellation — which targets the calibration block should present

> **This is a simulation, not a rig measurement, and must never be cited as one.**
> There is no eye tracker, no animal and no camera. The forward model from gaze angle
> to raw Purkinje vector is an *assumption* — stated in the script, swept across four
> optics settings, and replaced by bench data at V3. **No degree value below is a claim
> about our hardware.** What the numbers do support is a *ranking* between
> constellations, and every ranking here is reported across the whole sweep so it is
> visible whether it survives the assumption changing.
>
> The conditioning column is different in kind: it is `wl-preproc`'s own
> `_conditioning`, imported from their source rather than reimplemented, so that
> column is exactly what will gate a real session.

**Date:** 2026-09-05. **Script:** `tools/calibration_design.py`, seeded, deterministic.
**Geometry:** `Geometry(panel_diagonal_cm=80.01, viewing_distance_cm=57.0)` — S0's
stereoscope, whose per-eye viewport is half the panel's width and its full height.
**Against:** `wl-preproc` at `c3f6c5e`.

## What it settled

**A 3×3 grid at 75% reach, plus four intermediates on the diagonals at half that —
thirteen targets, all of them fit points.** Coordinates in section 7.

Four findings, in the order they change a decision:

1. **Reach dominates; count does not.** At equal animal cost, 9, 13 and 25 targets are
   indistinguishable (section 4). How far the targets reach changes error by 50%
   (section 3), and 75% of the field wins under every optics assumption. Pushing
   targets to the panel edge is *worse than* pulling them to 60%: the corners sit near
   21° eccentricity, outside the disc any task uses, and their leverage drags the
   quadratic away from where stimuli actually go.

2. **Conditioning cannot see extent, by construction.** A 3×3 shrunk to 60% of the
   field scores 0.2277 — identical to one spanning it — because the metric is
   scale-invariant on purpose. It then understates its own error by 3.0× against 1.5×
   for the full-field grid (section 2). **Conditioning is necessary and not
   sufficient**, and S5 §7's acceptance criterion needed a second clause.

3. **A ring plus a centre passes the gate while misreporting.** 0.1697 against a 0.10
   threshold — not degenerate, unlike the bare ring's 0.0000 — but two distinct radii
   leave the quadratic radial term resting on a single contrast. `docs/next-session.md`
   offered "a grid, or a ring plus centre" as equivalent; they are not.

4. **The thirteenth point buys survival, not accuracy.** Nine points fitting six
   parameters has three to spare: lose four targets and the second-order fit is not
   ill-conditioned, it is *impossible*, and the session falls back to affine at roughly
   2.5× the error. Thirteen points survive losing five, 95% of the time (section 5).
   For an animal that will not work every target, that is the entire argument.

**What no constellation fixes.** Around 0.13 deg RMS of bias survives every design here,
because the dominant nonlinearity of a Purkinje-difference signal is odd and radial
(`theta = arcsin(r/k)`) and `[1, dx, dy, dx², dy², dx·dy]` contains no odd radial term.
More targets cannot reach it. If V3 measures it larger than this, the answer is a basis
change, and the basis is `wl-preproc`'s to change, not ours.

**One idea measured down.** Holding four of the thirteen out as a validation set was the
first proposal. Section 6 shows the held-out estimate overstates true error by 34–51%
with a spread of ±0.05 deg — its own noise floor is the size of the quantity it is
meant to measure — and it is wrong by a similar margin whether the model fits or not,
so it does not detect misfit either. Recorded because the idea is an attractive one and
will otherwise be proposed again.

## 6. Why the intermediates are fit points, not held-out points

Holding four of the thirteen out to estimate error was proposed and then
measured down. Four points at ten fixations each carry a noise floor of
about 0.11 deg, which is the same size as the error being estimated, so the
estimate cannot resolve it. `spread` is the standard deviation of the
held-out estimate across repeats of the SAME calibration: a gate whose
reading moves that much between identical sessions cannot be acted on.

| optics | held-out estimate | spread | true RMS | error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| near-axial | 0.189 | +/-0.047 | 0.130 | +45% |
| moderate | 0.184 | +/-0.050 | 0.138 | +34% |
| strong obliquity | 0.192 | +/-0.055 | 0.131 | +46% |
| radial-dominated | 0.202 | +/-0.059 | 0.134 | +51% |

The estimate is wrong by a similar margin whether the model fits the
optics or not, so the gap between residual and held-out does not
discriminate misfit either -- which was the reason to hold them out.
They do more good inside the fit, where section 5 shows what they buy.
